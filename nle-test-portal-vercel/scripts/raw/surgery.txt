Q1. A 68-year-old man with atrial fibrillation develops sudden severe abdominal pain with minimal tenderness. Six hours later he develops guarding, metabolic acidosis and rising lactate. CT angiography shows an SMA embolus with bowel ischemia. What is the most appropriate management?
A. Elective right hemicolectomy after 48 hours
B. Emergency laparotomy with revascularization and assessment of bowel viability
C. Hartmann procedure without restoring mesenteric flow
D. Conservative treatment with IV fluids only
E. Laparoscopic cholecystectomy
Answer: B.
Explanation: Peritonitis indicates possible bowel infarction. Surgical exploration allows revascularization and resection of nonviable bowel.

Q2. A 59-year-old man has progressive dysphagia, initially to solids and later to liquids, with significant weight loss. Endoscopy demonstrates a resectable distal esophageal carcinoma without distant metastases. Which operation is most appropriate?
A. Heller myotomy
B. Ivor Lewis esophagectomy
C. Whipple procedure
D. Hartmann procedure
E. Total gastrectomy
Answer: B.
Explanation: Resectable distal esophageal carcinoma can be treated with Ivor Lewis esophagectomy, involving abdominal and right thoracic approaches with gastric pull-up.

Q3. A 55-year-old woman develops painless jaundice, pruritus and weight loss. CT shows a 3-cm mass in the pancreatic head without distant metastases or major vascular invasion. Which operation offers potentially curative treatment?
A. Distal pancreatectomy
B. Whipple pancreaticoduodenectomy
C. Total gastrectomy
D. Right hemicolectomy
E. Splenectomy
Answer: B.
Explanation: A resectable carcinoma in the head of pancreas is treated with pancreaticoduodenectomy.

Q4. A 63-year-old man presents with fever, jaundice, RUQ pain and hypotension. Ultrasound shows a dilated CBD containing a stone. After IV fluids and antibiotics, he remains septic. What is the most appropriate next step?
A. Elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy alone
B. Urgent ERCP for biliary decompression
C. Whipple procedure
D. Right hemicolectomy
E. Percutaneous nephrostomy
Answer: B.
Explanation: Severe obstructive cholangitis requires urgent biliary drainage, usually by ERCP, in addition to antibiotics and resuscitation.

Q5. A 71-year-old man presents with abdominal distension, vomiting and constipation. X-ray shows a coffee-bean sign. He has no peritonitis or evidence of perforation. What is the best initial treatment?
A. Immediate laparotomy and colectomy
B. Endoscopic decompression
C. Hartmann procedure immediately
D. Right hemicolectomy
E. Total colectomy
Answer: B.
Explanation: Uncomplicated sigmoid volvulus is initially treated with endoscopic decompression. Definitive sigmoid colectomy is generally performed later because recurrence is common.

Q6. A 46-year-old woman develops persistent RUQ pain, fever and leukocytosis. Ultrasound shows gallstones, gallbladder wall thickening and pericholecystic fluid. She is medically fit for surgery. What is the preferred definitive treatment?

Q7. A 62-year-old man develops generalized peritonitis after perforation of a sigmoid diverticulum. CT shows fecal contamination throughout the peritoneal cavity, and he is hypotensive despite resuscitation. Which operation is traditionally appropriate for this unstable patient?

Q8. A 55-year-old man has progressive dysphagia to both solids and liquids from the beginning. Endoscopy excludes malignancy, and manometry demonstrates absent peristalsis with impaired LES relaxation. Which procedure is most appropriate?
